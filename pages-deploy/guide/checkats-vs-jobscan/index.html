CheckATS vs Jobscan: Free ATS Checker Comparison (2026)

CheckATS · Updated October 17, 2026

Jobscan is the best-known name in ATS resume checking; CheckATS is the free, no-signup alternative with a published scoring formula. This comparison states what each tool actually gives you, admits where each one wins, and cites a source and date for every claim about Jobscan.

The short answer: Jobscan's free plan gives you 5 resume-vs-JD scans a month with an account; unlimited scans and its AI tools cost $49.95/month or $89.95/quarter. CheckATS gives its core JD-vs-resume check free with no signup and no scan cap, and publishes its whole scoring formula. Jobscan wins on depth and breadth; CheckATS wins on zero friction and transparency.

Is Jobscan free — and what does CheckATS give you for free?

Jobscan is partially free. Its free plan includes 5 resume scans per month, each comparing your resume against one job description, plus 5 ATS/recruiter findings and a resume builder you can use and download from without paying (resumefast.io, checked October 6, 2026; careery.pro, checked October 9, 2026). You do need to create an account, and once the 5 scans are used, the next check costs money.

Paid plans, per the same sources: $49.95 per month, or $89.95 every three months (about $29.98/month). The 7-day free trial applies only to the quarterly plan — it bills automatically unless you cancel — and there is no trial on the monthly plan. That trial structure is also stated in the FAQ on Jobscan's own pricing page (jobscan.co/pricing, checked October 17, 2026). Paid plans add unlimited scans plus AI tools: AI resume optimization, an AI cover letter generator, an AI bullet point generator, LinkedIn optimization, and job matching.

CheckATS takes the opposite approach to the free tier. The core check — paste resume, paste job description, get a simulated ATS score with a keyword-by-keyword breakdown — is free with no account and no monthly scan limit. The AI features also have free daily quotas: 5 AI reviews and 2 AI rewrites per day, resetting at UTC midnight. There is no account system at all, so there is nothing to sign up for.

How does each tool calculate its score?

Jobscan does not publish its scoring formula. You get a match-rate percentage with keyword gaps, formatting findings, and recruiter-style checks, and independent reviewers consistently describe its JD keyword analysis as among the deepest in the category (toolsforhumans.ai FAQ, cited in third-party comparisons). It also offers guidance tuned to specific ATS products such as Workday and Taleo. The tradeoff is opacity: you see findings, not the math behind them.

CheckATS publishes the entire formula on its methodology page: 60% exact JD keyword coverage plus 40% plain-text parse checks (contact info, date formats, encoding, length). Matching is case-insensitive but exact — "K8s" does not match "Kubernetes" — and generic soft-skill words are excluded from keyword counting. You can recompute the score by hand. The tradeoff is simplicity: it simulates keyword coverage and text extraction, not any employer's real screening rules.

Neither score predicts hiring. A match rate measures how well your wording overlaps with a posting; it cannot see recruiter settings, knockout questions, or the human who reads your resume next.

Where does Jobscan genuinely win?

Credit where it is due. Jobscan has been operating since about 2014, making it one of the oldest tools in this category, with the largest content library and user community around ATS optimization. Reviewers repeatedly call its reports the most detailed of the mainstream checkers, and its guidance for specific ATS platforms (Workday, Taleo) is something CheckATS does not attempt. If you want one paid subscription that covers scanning, a resume builder, cover letters, LinkedIn, and application tracking, Jobscan is the fuller toolkit — CheckATS has no builder, no tracker, and no LinkedIn or cover letter features, and this comparison will not pretend otherwise.

Where does CheckATS genuinely win?

Three things, all verifiable. First, zero friction: no account, no email, no monthly scan cap on the core check — paste and score in seconds. Second, transparency: the 60/40 formula is published and hand-checkable, and the tool is upfront about its limits (it simulates keyword coverage and text extraction, not real employer screening). Third, the JD-targeted comparison that Jobscan rations at 5 scans a month is CheckATS's free core feature, and its AI rewrite shows a word-by-word diff so you can see exactly what changed before you accept it.

Which one should you use for your job search?

Use CheckATS first when you want a fast, free, no-signup answer to one question: "which JD keywords am I actually missing?" Its published formula makes the result easy to sanity-check. Use Jobscan's free plan when you want a second opinion from its deeper keyword analysis, or consider the paid plan if you will genuinely use the whole toolkit — builder, cover letters, LinkedIn, tracker — for an active search of several weeks. Many job seekers can reasonably use both: CheckATS for quick unlimited checks while tailoring each application, Jobscan's 5 monthly scans for the applications that matter most. Whichever you pick, treat the score as a writing aid, not a verdict.

Frequently asked questions

Does Jobscan have a free plan?

Yes. Jobscan's free plan includes 5 resume-vs-job-description scans per month plus a free resume builder, but it requires an account. Unlimited scans, AI rewriting, the cover letter generator, and LinkedIn optimization require a paid subscription (jobscan.co/pricing and independent reviews, checked October 2026).

Is CheckATS really free with no signup?

The core ATS check is: paste your resume and a job description and get a score with no account and no monthly scan limit. The AI review (5 per day) and AI rewrite (2 per day) also have free daily quotas that reset at UTC midnight.

Will a high score on either tool guarantee an interview?

No. Both scores simulate keyword coverage and formatting; they cannot see recruiter settings, knockout questions, or the human reading your resume. Treat any match rate as a writing aid, not a probability of getting hired.

Can I use both CheckATS and Jobscan together?

Yes. A reasonable workflow: run your resume through CheckATS for a quick, no-signup keyword check with a published formula, then use Jobscan's free monthly scans for a second opinion from its deeper keyword analysis before you apply.
