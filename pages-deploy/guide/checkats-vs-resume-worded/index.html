CheckATS vs Resume Worded: Which Free ATS Check Shows Its Work (2026)

CheckATS · Updated October 23, 2026

Resume Worded is famous for pinning every resume flag to the exact line; CheckATS is the free, no-signup alternative that publishes its entire scoring formula. This checkats vs resume worded comparison states what each tool actually gives you — admitting where each one wins — with a source and date for every claim about Resume Worded.

The short answer: Resume Worded's free account gives a partial score and limited feedback; the full line-by-line report, complete JD targeting, and full LinkedIn review are Pro-only — $49/month, $99/quarter, $229/year as of October 2026. CheckATS's core check is free with no account and no scan cap, and its whole formula is public. Resume Worded wins on depth; CheckATS wins on zero friction and transparency.

What does Resume Worded's free tier actually include?

A partial score and limited feedback, after you create a free account. The full Score My Resume report — including the line-by-line analysis — the complete Targeted Resume comparison, and the full LinkedIn review are Pro-only (official Pro page, checked October 23, 2026).

The free tier is real but deliberately incomplete: an instant score plus the headline fixes. The line-by-line engine, the full Targeted Resume report, the full LinkedIn analysis, and the ATS templates unlock only with Pro (resumeworded.com/get-pro, checked October 23, 2026; thetoolsverse.com, September 9, 2026). Even the free tier requires an account — the signup gate is the price of entry.

Pro is sold on three billing terms: $49/month month-to-month, $99 every three months (about $33/month), or $229 once a year (about $19/month), all cancellable at any time. One honest-reading note: many roundups quote "$19/month," but that is the annual rate paid up front — starting Pro without a commitment costs $49 (thetoolsverse.com, September 9, 2026; scale.jobs, October 2026).

CheckATS inverts this structure: the core check — paste resume, paste job description, get a simulated ATS score with a keyword-by-keyword breakdown — costs nothing, needs no account, and has no monthly scan cap. The AI features also carry free daily quotas: 5 reviews and 2 rewrites per day, resetting at UTC midnight.

How does each tool calculate its score?

Resume Worded grades your resume against 30+ recruiter checks and pins every flag to the exact line, but it does not publish how those checks are weighted into the score. CheckATS publishes its entire formula — 60% exact JD keyword coverage plus 40% plain-text parse checks — on its methodology page, so you can recompute the result by hand. Neither score can see an employer's real screening rules.

Resume Worded describes a score "built from the checklists recruiters and hiring managers actually screen with" — deterministic for the same resume, covering contact details, length, structure, keyword targeting, and more (resumeworded.com, checked October 23, 2026). It does not show the weighting: you see the flags, not the math behind the number. That is a deliberate product choice — opacity keeps the score hard to game.

CheckATS goes the other way: 60% exact, case-insensitive JD keyword coverage plus 40% plain-text parse checks (contact info, date formats, encoding, length). Matching is deliberately literal — "K8s" does not match "Kubernetes" — and generic soft-skill words are excluded. You can verify every point by hand. The tradeoff: it simulates keyword coverage and text extraction, not any employer's real screening rules.

The practical difference: with Resume Worded you get richer guidance and must trust the score; with CheckATS you get a simpler score you can audit. One caveat for both: a match rate measures wording overlap, not hireability — it cannot see knockout questions, recruiter settings, or the human reading your resume next.

What can you actually do in each tool?

Resume Worded's full toolkit — line-by-line analysis, complete JD targeting, the full LinkedIn review, the AI writer, recruiter-vetted templates — is Pro-only; the free account gives the score and headline fixes. CheckATS's core check is entirely free with no signup, but it has no LinkedIn review, job tracker, or cover letter generator.

Where does Resume Worded genuinely win?

Resume Worded wins on feedback depth and breadth: 30+ recruiter checks with every flag pinned to the exact line, an AI fixer that rewrites each weak bullet from your own experience, a full LinkedIn profile review, and recruiter-vetted ATS templates — dimensions where CheckATS is thinner or entirely absent.

The line-by-line analysis is the signature feature for a reason: each flagged line gets the reason named, and the AI fixer drafts a replacement from your own experience that you approve line by line (resumeworded.com, checked October 23, 2026). For someone rebuilding a resume from scratch, that guided loop is genuinely more useful than a keyword gap list alone.

The LinkedIn review is a second real advantage: tailored feedback on the profile recruiters see before your resume. CheckATS has nothing comparable, and this checkats vs resume worded comparison will not pretend otherwise. Add recruiter-vetted ATS templates and sample bullets, and the paid toolkit is a fuller package for an intensive search.

Where does CheckATS genuinely win?

CheckATS wins on zero friction and transparency: the core check needs no account and has no scan cap, the entire 60/40 formula is published and hand-checkable, the score is labeled a simulation rather than a verdict, and the AI rewrite shows a word-by-word diff.

Three things, all verifiable. First, friction: no account, no email, no scan limit — paste and score in under a minute. Second, transparency: the published formula means a given score means the same thing to everyone, and generic soft-skill words are excluded. Third, the JD-targeted comparison Resume Worded reserves for Pro is CheckATS's free core feature — with a word-level diff on the free AI rewrite.

Plainly stated: CheckATS has no LinkedIn review, no cover letter generator, and no job tracker. If those are what you need, the honest recommendation is a tool that has them — starting with Resume Worded's own free tier.

Which one should you use for your job search?

Start with CheckATS for a fast, free, no-signup answer to "which JD keywords am I missing?" — its published formula is easy to sanity-check. Use Resume Worded's free tier for a second opinion, or Pro for the line-by-line rewrite loop, the LinkedIn review, and the templates.

Concretely: run each application through CheckATS first — it costs nothing and never runs out — then use Resume Worded's free score as a second opinion for the roles that matter most, or take a Pro month for the deep rewrite pass. Just do not chase either score to 100 by stuffing keywords: a resume optimized for a number fails at the only stage that matters — the human reader.

Frequently asked questions

Is Resume Worded free?

Yes, with limits: a free account returns an instant score and limited feedback, but the full line-by-line report, the complete Targeted Resume comparison, and the full LinkedIn review require Pro — $49/month, $99/quarter, or $229/year as of October 2026. Even the free tier requires an account.

Do I need an account to use CheckATS?

No. There is no account system at all: paste your resume and the job description, and the core check runs free with no monthly scan limit. The AI review (5 per day) and AI rewrite (2 per day) also have free daily quotas that reset at UTC midnight.

Which tool shows you how its score is calculated?

CheckATS publishes its entire formula — 60% exact JD keyword coverage plus 40% plain-text parse checks — so you can recompute the score by hand. Resume Worded pins every flag to the exact line, but does not publish how the checks are weighted.

Will a high score on either tool guarantee an interview?

No. Both scores are simulations of wording overlap and formatting; they cannot see employer knockout questions, recruiter settings, or the human who reads your resume next. Treat any score as a writing aid, not a probability of getting hired.
