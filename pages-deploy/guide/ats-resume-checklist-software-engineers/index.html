ATS Resume Checklist for Software Engineers (2026)

ATS Resume Optimizer · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Most "ATS rejections" aren't rejections at all — but keyword mismatch and unparseable formatting really do bury good engineers. This 10-point checklist covers what actually matters before you hit submit.

The 10-point checklist

  1. Mirror the job description's exact words

    ATS keyword matching is literal. If the JD says "Kubernetes," write "Kubernetes" — "K8s" alone won't match. If it says "AWS," don't rely on "Amazon Web Services" alone. Include both the acronym and the full term the first time you use them.

  2. Keep the layout to a single column of plain text

    Multi-column layouts, tables, text boxes, and graphics get read out of order — or skipped entirely — by resume parsers. Fancy does not mean parseable. One column, top to bottom.

  3. Use standard section headers

    "Experience," "Skills," "Education." Creative headers like "Where I've Made an Impact" confuse parsers that look for conventional labels.

  4. Put a dedicated Skills section near the top

    List exact technology names — languages, frameworks, cloud platforms, tools — spelled exactly as they appear in job posts. This is the highest-density keyword area of your resume.

  5. Quantify everything a hiring manager would care about

    "Reduced p99 latency by 40%," "led migration serving 2M daily users," "cut deploy time from 2 hours to 15 minutes." Numbers are the fastest credibility signal for both parsers and humans.

  6. Keep dates in one consistent format

    "Jan 2021 – Mar 2023" everywhere. Mixing "01/2021," "2021-01," and "Jan 2021" looks sloppy to parsers and humans alike, and gaps become harder to explain.

  7. Make contact info machine-readable

    Email and phone on their own lines in plain text — not inside a header image, icon, or graphic. If a parser can't extract your email, nothing else matters.

  8. Submit as a text-based PDF or DOCX

    Never a scanned or image-based PDF. Quick test: if you can't select the text with your mouse, neither can the parser.

  9. Tailor one resume per application

    One generic resume keyword-matched to nothing loses to a tailored resume every time. Swap the skills emphasis and reword 2–3 bullets per JD — small effort, large difference in keyword coverage.

  10. Run an ATS check before you submit

    Paste your resume and the JD into a checker and confirm the must-have keywords actually appear. Thirty seconds of verification beats wondering why you never heard back.

Frequently asked questions

Do recruiters really use ATS to auto-reject resumes?

Short answer: the famous "75% of resumes are auto-rejected" statistic is a marketing myth dating back to 2012 with no reliable methodology. Real automatic rejection comes from knockout questions (work authorization, location, minimum years). But keyword search, ranking, and filtering inside the ATS are real — which is why keyword coverage still matters.

Should software engineers use a fancy resume template?

For online applications: no. Keep one clean, single-column version for ATS portals, and keep the designed version for handing directly to humans at meetups or via referral.

How many JD keywords are enough?

Cover the must-haves first. Chasing 100% coverage on nice-to-haves has diminishing returns — and stuffing keywords where they don't belong reads badly to the human who eventually reviews your resume.
