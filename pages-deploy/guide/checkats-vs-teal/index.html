CheckATS vs Teal: No-Signup ATS Check vs All-in-One Tracker (2026)

CheckATS · Updated October 26, 2026

Teal is a whole job-search workspace — tracker, resume builder, and Chrome extension in one place — while CheckATS does one thing: a free, no-signup check of your resume against a job description. This checkats vs teal comparison states what each tool actually gives you, admits where each one wins, and sources every claim about Teal.

The short answer: Teal's free plan is genuinely generous — unlimited resumes, unlimited tracking, and 10 templates — but its full keyword list and unlimited AI generation sit behind Teal+, at $13 every 7 days, $29 every 30 days, or $79 every 90 days (official pricing page, October 26, 2026). CheckATS's core check is free with no account and no scan cap, and its formula is public. Teal wins on managing a search; CheckATS wins on a single check.

What does Teal's free plan actually include?

Teal's free plan covers the whole job-search workflow except its AI power features: unlimited resumes and unlimited job tracking, 10 templates, the top 5 keywords from a job description, basic analysis, and fixed, non-renewable AI credits — 10 for bullets, 2 for summaries, 2 for cover letters (tealhq.com/pricing, checked October 26, 2026).

That free tier is the most complete in this comparison series: save jobs with the Chrome extension, keep a tailored version attached to each application, and track every stage from applied to offer — all without paying. No credit card is required to start, and you can upgrade, downgrade, or cancel at any time (tealhq.com/pricing, checked October 26, 2026).

The limits matter as much as the generosity. Free keyword matching shows only the top 5 keywords from a job description, not the full gap list. Free analysis covers basic issues only; advanced issues are a Teal+ feature. And the AI credits do not renew: once the 10 bullet, 2 summary, and 2 cover letter credits are spent, further AI generation requires Teal+ (tealhq.com/pricing, checked October 26, 2026).

Structural difference: Teal is an account-based workspace, so even the free plan requires an account; CheckATS's core check has no account system at all.

What does Teal+ cost, and what does it unlock?

Teal+ is billed in three cycles on the official pricing page: $13 every 7 days (about $1.85 a day), $29 every 30 days (about $0.96 a day), or $79 every 90 days (about $0.87 a day) — so do the per-day math before you pick (tealhq.com/pricing, checked October 26, 2026).

Teal+ unlocks unlimited AI generation across bullets, summaries, and cover letters; the full keyword list instead of just the top 5; advanced Analysis Mode issues; unlimited resume and email templates; and advanced Design Mode settings (tealhq.com/pricing, checked October 26, 2026).

Two honest-reading notes. First, Teal's prices have been reported inconsistently across 2026: older reviews cite about $9 a week and an annual plan near $179 a year, but the official page checked on October 26, 2026 lists only the three cycles above — no annual option. Second, the weekly plan is a trap if left running: $13 a week works out to roughly $56 a month, nearly double the $29 monthly plan. Prices change — verify before paying.

CheckATS inverts this shape: the core check is free, unlimited, and account-free, with free daily AI quotas — 5 reviews and 2 rewrites per day, resetting at UTC midnight.

How does each tool check your resume?

Teal checks your resume with issue-based feedback: a keyword list from the job description (top 5 free, full list on Teal+) and an analysis mode (basic free, advanced on Teal+). CheckATS checks it with one published score: 60% exact JD keyword coverage plus 40% plain-text parse checks.

Where does Teal genuinely win?

Teal wins on running the whole search in one place: unlimited job tracking with a Chrome extension that saves postings in one click, unlimited tailored resume versions attached to each application, a contact manager for networking, and email templates per job stage — a dimension where CheckATS simply does not compete.

The tracker is the core advantage. For someone applying to dozens of roles, the chaos is twenty applications at different stages, each with its own tailored version and follow-up date. Teal's free tier replaces the spreadsheet for that workflow, and the Chrome extension removes the friction of saving each posting (tealhq.com/pricing, checked October 26, 2026).

The second real advantage is the guided rewrite loop on Teal+: unlimited AI bullets, summaries, and cover letters, plus the full keyword list and advanced analysis, all inside the same workspace where the resume already lives. CheckATS has no tracker, no multi-version resume management, and no Chrome extension, and this checkats vs teal comparison will not pretend otherwise.

Where does CheckATS genuinely win?

CheckATS wins on zero friction and transparency for a single check: no account, no scan cap, the entire 60/40 formula published and hand-checkable, the score labeled a simulation rather than a verdict, and a word-by-word diff on the free AI rewrite.

Three things, all verifiable. First, friction: no account, no email, no scan limit — paste your resume and the job description and see the full keyword gap list in under a minute, including the terms Teal's free plan cuts off after the top 5. Second, transparency: the published formula means a score means the same thing to everyone. Third, the full JD-targeted comparison Teal reserves for Teal+ is CheckATS's free core feature, with a word-level diff on the free AI rewrite.

Plainly stated: CheckATS has no job tracker, no Chrome extension, no multi-version resume library, and no cover letter generator. If those are what you need, start with Teal's own free tier — it costs nothing to try.

Which one should you use for your job search?

Use both, for different jobs: run each application through CheckATS first for a fast, free, no-signup answer to "which JD keywords am I missing?", then manage the search in Teal's free tracker. This checkats vs teal verdict: pay for Teal+ only if you will use its unlimited AI generation and full keyword list.

Concretely: paste each posting into CheckATS before you tailor the resume — it costs nothing and never runs out — then save the role in Teal, attach the tailored version, and log the follow-up. Take Teal+ for a month in a heavy application sprint; skip the weekly plan unless the sprint is one week long, since $13 a week becomes about $56 a month. Do not chase any score to 100 by stuffing keywords: a resume optimized for a number fails with the human reader.

Frequently asked questions

Is Teal free?

Yes, with limits: unlimited resumes and unlimited job tracking, 10 templates, the top 5 keywords from a job description, and fixed AI credits (10 bullets, 2 summaries, 2 cover letters). Teal+ costs $13 every 7 days, $29 every 30 days, or $79 every 90 days (official pricing page, October 26, 2026). A free account is required; no credit card is needed to start.

Do I need an account to use CheckATS?

No. The core check needs no account at all: paste your resume and the job description, and it runs free with no monthly scan limit. AI features use points for logged-in users (10 per review, 20 per rewrite); anonymous visitors have daily IP quotas (5 reviews, 2 rewrites per UTC day).

Which tool shows you how its score is calculated?

CheckATS publishes its entire formula — 60% exact JD keyword coverage plus 40% plain-text parse checks — so you can recompute the score by hand. Teal gives issue-based feedback instead: a keyword list (top 5 on the free plan, full list on Teal+) and basic or advanced resume analysis, not a single published score formula.

Can I use both tools together?

Yes, and that is the setup this comparison recommends: run each application through CheckATS first for a free, no-signup keyword gap check, then manage the search in Teal's free tracker. Pay for Teal+ only if you will use its unlimited AI generation and full keyword list.
